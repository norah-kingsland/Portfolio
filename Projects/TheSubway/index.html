The Subway

This was a personal project undertaken to explore a retrofuturistic, Cyberpunk-inspired environment. Over the course of one week, I created a stylized train station scene in Blender.

I completed all aspects of the project independently, researching inspiration, modelling, texturing, lighting, and rendering it. I researched shader techniques and procedural workflows to achieve realistic textures and atmosphere.

Defining Success

Creating this project, I wanted to create a visually compelling scene that balanced aesthetic detail with efficient modelling for rendering performance. I wanted to create key objects without relying solely on tutorials, ensuring each object fit into the environment. My primary goal was to build a scene that used procedural materials, custom shaders, and efficient modelling techniques to developing my personal style and create something cool.

My moodboard / inspiration for my intended cyberpunk subway aesthetic.

Setting the Scene

I started out by blocking in my environment, using a human model from BlenderKit for scale. Excited to jump in to the neon aesthetic, BlenderGuru's neon sign tutorial provided help creating a neon shop sign, equipped with a custom shader.

Progress after the first day working on the project: the platform is blocked in, with the neon sign textured and the meat shelf from BlenderKit added to test if it filled out the space enough (I would later modify the asset).

Modeling & Texturing:

I created procedural materials and custom shaders for many of the objects in the scene. For objects such as the vending machines and furniture, I modified BlenderKit assets to fit the style, adjusting UVs, textures, and adding emission lighting. One of my favourite materials to create was the shop's neon "Supernova" sign, which uses emission to give it a glowing appearance from the inside whilst "dipping" the back half of the sign in a matte black as you would see in a real neon sign.

Custom shader for neon sign, inspired by Blender Guru.

Weathering, Lighting, and Environment

Adding dirt and unevenness brought the scene to life. Almost every material has a brown mix shader with its opacity driven by a noise pattern. This further sells the worn down look of the subway while making textures look less repetitive, and it was really fun to experiment with. For high-wear areas such as the baseboards, ambient occlusion and manual painting gave them an extra layer of grime.

I lit the scene to give it a "glowing from within" atmosphere: Most of the lighting sources are not white, which adds to the retrofuturistic vibe. Environmental details such as the wet concrete floors, using noise textures and the roughness slider to simulate puddles, gave a touch of rugged realism.

The back corner of the environment: while dimly lit, ambient occlusion on the base boards and texture painted seats add the appearance of wear.

Outcomes and Takeaways

The final scene features a cohesive retrofuturistic station with realistic textures, and atmospheric lighting. I measured success visually: the scene conveyed the intended mood and style, objects appeared integrated and polished, and I was proud of what I had created. I got the project up to a good standard in less time than I had expected (about 4 half-days of working on it), so I had time left in my chosen timeframe to iterate on some of the designs, improving realism and render efficiency.

I'm particularly happy with the results of the wet concrete floor, which took a lot of tweaking to get right, as well as the train, which I textured from scratch using photos to make custom shaders, and UV-mapped creatively from less than ideal reference images.

This project deepened my understanding of procedural texturing, shader customization, and efficient modelling. I learned how to balance aesthetic goals with technical constraints, and how small iterative changes (like adding dirt mix shaders or adjusting UVs) can significantly improve realism. For future projects, I’d like to learn more about techniques to reduce visual noise in low-light renders and how to further optimize scene lighting for both realism and render performance. Additionally, I'd like to utilise the more "proper" UV unwrapping techniques I have learned since, as this was my first experience texturing from scratch and I was teaching myself as I went along.
