The Subway

With foundational Blender skills under my belt, this was my first undertaking without explicitly following a tutorial. I took the idea of a retrofuturistic society with lots of inspiration from Cyberpunk 2077 and wanted to create a train station that fit this style. Over the course of this project I learned a lot about procedural material creation and custom shaders, as well as efficient UV unwrapping, different modifiers, lighting, and how to really sell a scene(for example, most textures have a dirt texture overlaid on top of them, using either a noise texture or weight painting to drive where the dirt is visible). This was my first project focusing on more than one key object, so polygon count mattered and I had to get creative with modifiers and other techniques to make things render faster. I used BlenderKit assets for some items in the scene, such as the vending machines and storefront furniture, but almost everything was modified in one way or another: for example, the vending machines required a lot of editing to both the UV maps, textures, and emission lighting to fit the scene.


I'm particularly proud of the wet concrete floor of the station, which uses a standard concrete texture with the roughness and displacement driven by a noise texture to give the illusion of puddles. It took a lot of fiddling to get right. Additionally, the train was my first time modelling something entirely without a tutorial or "perfect" reference image(s), its texture is made up of a photo of the side of a train heavily distorted to fit the UVs.


In the future, I'd love to learn more about how to fix the slight static effect that came across in these puddles in the rendered video. From my research, I'm under the impression that it is an inevitable result of low lighting in scenes, as well as not having enough samples in the render. Increasing the samples helped a lot with the effect compared to how it looked in my initial render, but increasing the lighting did not seem to help, and I'm sure there are other solutions that can be done that I'd love to learn about. Overall, however, I am very proud of this project and how it shows off my personal style.

Abandoned House

This project has been my first experience diving a little deeper into some of Blender's more complex features. I followed Blender Guru's abandoned house tutorial, and learned a lot about shader nodes, UV editing, and texture painting. This project was great practice building more complex meshes using the simple geometry building blocks and modifiers that blender provides. The decorative gable ornaments under the peaks of the roof, for example, seems simple but caused me a lot of frustration at the time,

The textures of the wooden walls use the RGB channels on an image mask to correspond to different shaders. This effect, paired with a color burn color mixer, allows the wood texture to almost seamlessly blend between painted wood, peeled wood, and moss, as well as a water damaged overlay. This technique was also used on the house foundations, porch, ground, and roof to add depth to an otherwise flat texture. This technique was incredibly useful to learn, and I still regularly use it to add depth to textures. In addition, I explored the basics of particle systems to create the environment around the house, layering different kinds of grass and trees to add depth.
